# How to Contribute

Thank you for helping build a starting-point vibe-coding policy template. This guide assumes no technical background.

## Before you start

1. **Everything is public, immediately.** Your edits are visible to anyone as soon as you submit them, before the maintainer reviews them. Closing or editing a submission afterward does not reliably hide what you posted.
2. **Your GitHub username is attached** to everything you submit. If you contribute as part of your job, confirm your employer allows public, attributed input and allows you to share it under the license below. Consider which GitHub account you use.
3. **Keep it confidential-safe.** Do not include:
   - Names of people, residents, employees, or customers
   - Names of your organization's internal systems, vendors, or projects
   - Internal URLs, credentials, or non-public documents
   - Stories someone could trace back to a specific incident

   Describe situations in general terms. Instead of "the permit system at [City] leaked records in March," write "an internal tool built to speed up permit lookups exposed records to unintended users."
4. **Link, don't paste,** material you do not have the right to share, such as other organizations' policies or copyrighted text.
5. **Submissions are suggestions.** The maintainer may shorten, merge, reword, or decline them, and may remove identifying details.
6. **License:** by contributing, you agree your contribution is shared under [CC BY 4.0](LICENSE).

## How to add your idea

You need a free account at [github.com](https://github.com). Always start from this repository's page, not from a copy of it.

1. Open the topic file you want to add to. Find it in the topic list in the [README](README.md).
2. Click the **pencil icon** (Edit this file) at the top right of the file.
3. If GitHub says you need to fork the repository, click **Fork this repository**. This is normal. It creates your own copy automatically.
4. Scroll to the **Group ideas** heading at the bottom. Add your entry **directly below it**, using this format:

   ```markdown
   ### Short title for your idea

   - **Type:** Risk / Idea / Example / Wording / Link
   - **Detail:** One to three sentences. Keep it general and free of identifying details.
   ```

5. Do not change any text above **Group ideas**.
6. Click **Commit changes...** (top right). In the message box, write a short summary, for example "Add example to Privacy topic." Click **Propose changes**.
7. On the next screen, click **Create pull request**. You may need to click it a second time on the following screen. In the description, name the topic and confirm that you removed identifying details.

You're done. The maintainer will review your pull request and may leave a comment asking for changes. GitHub will email you when something happens.

## Tips

- One idea per entry makes review easier.
- If your pull request shows a conflict message, do not worry and do not try to fix it. The maintainer will handle it.
- To add a second idea later, start again from step 1 on this repository's page.

## Questions, or changes to existing text

Open an issue: go to the **Issues** tab, click **New issue**, and describe your question or the change you suggest. Use this to propose changes to text above **Group ideas**.
